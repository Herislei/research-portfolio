Deployment-Aligned Evaluation with Temporal and Geographic Holdouts: A Cross-Case Empirical Study of Forecasting and Semantic Segmentation

Abstract

Machine-learning systems are often evaluated with data partitions that do not match the structure of intended deployment. This study examines how deployment-aligned holdouts constrain the generalization claims that can be supported across two heterogeneous applied-learning tasks. The paper does not estimate a formal magnitude of distribution shift and does not compare structured holdouts against random splitting; instead, it asks what evidence is defensible when time or geography is used as the unit of separation and the final holdout is excluded from development.

Case I uses 17,379 hourly Capital Bikeshare observations (2011–2012) under a conservative monthly temporal protocol. Among the reported forecasting configurations, a weather-enhanced Histogram Gradient Boosting model produced the lowest observed errors (MAE 36.59, RMSE 61.88, RMSLE 0.461) on 5,919 evaluation observations, a 52.6% lower MAE than the historical baseline under the same protocol. The preserved notebook also shows lower HGB-weather MAE than the baseline in every plotted monthly evaluation window.

Case II uses an author-defined cross-city split of the Inria Aerial Image Labeling dataset: Austin, Chicago, and Kitsap for training, Tyrol-W for validation, and labeled Vienna imagery as a held-out final evaluation city. A deterministic seed-42 U-Net/ResNet-34 execution achieved mean IoU 0.588, Dice 0.716, Precision 0.635, Recall 0.886, and Boundary F1 0.297 on 576 sampled Vienna patches. A sensitivity analysis restricted to the 543 patches containing building pixels produced similar region metrics, and retained qualitative failure cases expose substantial false positives and boundary errors.

The contribution is a bounded claim-evidence reporting discipline supported by preserved computational artifacts, not a new learning algorithm or a universal robustness result.

Overview

This cross-case empirical study examines deployment-aligned evaluation across two heterogeneous machine-learning settings: time-series forecasting under temporal holdouts and semantic segmentation under geographic holdouts.

The study emphasizes leakage control, reproducibility, failure analysis, transparent deployment assumptions, and bounded generalization claims. Rather than proposing a new learning algorithm, the work focuses on the relationship between evaluation design and the scientific claims that can reasonably be supported by model performance.

Research Cases

Case I — Time-series forecasting: Capital Bikeshare demand prediction using a conservative temporal evaluation protocol and machine-learning models including Histogram Gradient Boosting.

Case II — Semantic segmentation: Building-footprint segmentation using an author-defined cross-city split of the Inria Aerial Image Labeling Dataset, with Vienna retained as the final geographic holdout.

Reproducibility

The associated public repository contains the deterministic Jupyter notebook, Python workflow, data-loading logic, model configurations, random-state controls, evaluation definitions, and supporting documentation.
